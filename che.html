The Human Cost

Asthma

Chronic inflammation of the airways. High pollution levels trigger severe asthma attacks and are linked to the onset of the disease, particularly in children.

COPD

Chronic Obstructive Pulmonary Disease. Long-term exposure to vehicular pollutants is a major risk factor for developing this progressive lung disease.

Bronchitis

Inflammation of the bronchial tubes. Particulate matter and other pollutants irritate the airways, leading to acute and chronic bronchitis.

Forging a Path to Cleaner Air

Tackling Delhi's air pollution requires a multi-faceted approach involving policy changes, technological advancements, and public participation. Here are some of the key strategies being implemented to reduce vehicular emissions and improve public health.

⚡️

Electric Vehicle (EV) Policy

Promoting the adoption of electric vehicles through subsidies, tax waivers, and the development of a robust charging infrastructure to achieve zero tailpipe emissions.

🚌

Public Transport Augmentation

Expanding the Delhi Metro network and increasing the fleet of low-emission CNG and electric buses to provide viable alternatives to private vehicles.

⛽️

Leap to BS-VI Norms

Delhi has transitioned to stricter Bharat Stage VI emission standards for fuel and vehicles, significantly reducing permissible sulphur and nitrogen oxide levels.

📅

Odd-Even Scheme

A temporary road rationing measure implemented during periods of extreme pollution to reduce the number of private cars on the road by half.

🚶‍♀️

Non-Motorised Transport

Developing dedicated cycling tracks and pedestrian-friendly infrastructure to encourage walking and cycling for short-distance travel.

🍃

Green Corridors & Tree Planting

Increasing green cover along roadsides and in open spaces helps to naturally filter pollutants and reduce ambient dust levels.
